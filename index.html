Topic 1 · Whole numbers

Place value, rounding and order of operations

Every whole number is built from ones, tens, hundreds and thousands. Once you can see those pieces, rounding and multi-step problems get much easier.

  • Base tenEach place is worth 10 times the place to its right. The 4 in 2,435 means 4 hundreds, or 400.
  • RoundingFind the two round numbers on either side. Halfway or past it rounds up. Before halfway rounds down.
  • Order of operationsParentheses, then exponents, then multiply and divide from left to right, then add and subtract from left to right.

Topic 2 · Fractions

Equal parts of a whole

A fraction names equal parts. The bottom number (denominator) says how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number (numerator) says how many of those parts you have.

  • Equivalent fractionsMultiply or divide the top and bottom by the same number. The amount stays the same; only the size of the pieces changes.
  • Adding and subtractingThe pieces must be the same size first, so rewrite both fractions with a common denominator.
  • MultiplyingMultiply the tops, then multiply the bottoms. Simplify at the end.

Topic 3 · Decimals

Place value to the right of the point

Decimals keep the base-ten pattern going past the ones place: tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Each place is one tenth of the place to its left.

  • Reading decimals0.37 means 37 hundredths, the same as the fraction 37/100.
  • ComparingLine up the decimal points and compare from the left. More digits does not mean a bigger number.
  • Adding and subtractingLine up the decimal points, fill empty places with zeros, then work like whole numbers.

Topic 4 · Percents

Percent means “per hundred”

35% means 35 out of every 100. That is the fraction 35/100 and the decimal 0.35, three names for the same amount.

  • Percent of a numberChange the percent to a decimal, then multiply. 20% of 80 is 0.20 × 80 = 16.
  • Benchmarks50% is half. 25% is a quarter. 10% moves the decimal point one place left.
  • Finding the percentDivide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100.

Topic 5 · Proportional reasoning

Ratios, rates and proportions

A ratio compares two amounts, like 2 cups of red paint for every 3 cups of blue (2:3). A rate compares different units, like 150 miles in 3 hours.

  • Unit rateHow much for one. 150 miles in 3 hours is 150 ÷ 3 = 50 miles per hour.
  • ProportionTwo equal ratios, like 3/4 = 15/20. Whatever you multiply the bottom by, multiply the top by the same number.
  • Sharing in a ratioAdd the parts of the ratio to get the number of equal boxes, then divide the total by that number.

Topic 6 · Setting up equations

Keep the scale balanced

An equation says two sides are equal, like a balanced scale. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other side, and it stays balanced.

  • Undo in reverseTo solve 3x + 4 = 19, undo the + 4 first, then undo the × 3.
  • Inverse operationsSubtraction undoes addition. Division undoes multiplication. And the other way around.
  • Word problemsName the unknown with a letter, write what the words say as an equation, then solve and check.
